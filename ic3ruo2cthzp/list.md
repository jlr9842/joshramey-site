# Pool game shoot and sound list

Updated 2026-10-29

These photos, measurements and sounds help make the pool game look and sound like a real hall. Anyone can help: pick a section, tick each box as you get it, and add a note if something was hard to get. Nothing has to be perfect. A clear, even, glare-free photo beats a fancy one.

## Quick start (read this first)

1. **Ask the hall.** Say hi to the staff and check it's OK to take photos of a table. Shoot when nobody is playing nearby.
2. **No people, faces or logos** in any photo. Logos on the table itself are fine; they get painted out.
3. **Bring a sheet of plain white paper.** It tells us the true colour.
4. **Pick a section below** and work down its list.
5. **Send everything to Josh** afterwards (see the end of this part).

## The three kinds of photo

**Flat texture shots** (cloth, rail, wood, wall, floor). These are most of the list.

- Hold the camera flat and square to the surface, as if photographing a page. About 30 cm (a forearm) away.
- Turn the lamp on and look for shine. If you see a bright spot, tilt a little or shade it with your hand.
- Take one with the white paper lying in the shot, then the same shot without it.
- For big surfaces, take two or three shots that overlap by about a third.

**Shape shots** (pockets, lamp, trim, stool). Shoot straight on from the front, from the side, and straight down from above. Add a tape measure in the shot if you have one.

**Room shots.** Stand at the end of the table, camera at table height, and shoot down the table to the far wall. Then do the same from the other end.

## Camera settings

**iPhone (good for everything)**

- Use the Photos camera with the flash off. HEIC or ProRAW are both fine.
- Stay on the 1x lens. Don't use 0.5x, which bends straight lines. For close-ups, just move closer; the phone switches to macro by itself.
- Tap the surface to focus, then drag the sun icon down a little if the bright parts look washed out.

**Mirrorless (best for cloth, wood and the room shots)**

- Shoot RAW plus JPEG, with the flash off.
- Use a normal lens at about 35-50 mm. Avoid wide angles.
- Set f/8 and ISO 100-400. If the shutter speed drops below 1/60, brace on the rail or use a tripod.
- Leave white balance on auto, since the white paper shot fixes the colour later.

## Measurements and sounds

- **Measurements:** use a tape measure, then send the numbers to Josh (or photograph the tape against the item).
- **Sounds:** these are recorded in the Pool Position game itself (Play › the game › Tune › Record sounds from your table), on Josh's phone or yours if you have the test version. Find a quiet moment and take five or more of each.

## Sending them over

- **iPhone:** select the photos › Share › Options › turn on All Photos Data › AirDrop to Josh. This keeps the full-quality originals.
- **Mirrorless:** copy the card to a computer, then AirDrop the RAW files to Josh or drop them in a shared folder.
- Tick the boxes on this page for what you got, so nobody shoots it twice.

## Sounds to record

The rail, jaw and pocket sounds are made up by the computer today, so they matter most. Ball clicks, the cue tip and the break are free stand-in recordings. Take five or more of each; the capture cuts them into single hits.

**Most needed**

- [ ] Rail (cushion) hits: soft, medium, hard.
- [ ] Ball rattling in the jaws of a corner pocket, and of a side pocket.
- [ ] Ball dropping into a corner pocket, and into a side pocket.
- [ ] Ball rolling down the return and knocking into the others.

**Replace the stand-ins**

- [ ] Ball-on-ball clicks: soft, medium, hard.
- [ ] Cue tip on the cue ball: soft and hard, plus a miscue if one happens.
- [ ] The break, from the head of the table.

**Nice to have**

- [ ] Chalking the tip.
- [ ] Racking: balls into the rack, and the rack lifted off.
- [ ] Ball rolling slowly across the cloth.
- [ ] Cue laid on the rail.
- [ ] 30 s of room sound (a quiet moment, no clear voices).

## 3D scans (iPhone Pro with LiDAR)

A Pro iPhone can scan a whole room in a minute and turn small objects into textured 3D models. Scans give the real layout and shapes; they get rebuilt as light, clean models for the game, so they don't need to be perfect.

**How to scan an object** (Reality Composer, Apple's free app › Object Capture)

- Put the object on a plain, matte surface in even light. Walk slowly around it in three rings: low, middle, high.
- Matte things scan best. Black, shiny or chrome parts come out patchy, so take the photos above as well.

**How to scan a room** (a free room-scanning app such as 3D Scanner App or Polycam, Room mode; it uses Apple's RoomPlan)

- Walk the room slowly, pointing at each wall, the floor edges and the doors. Wait until nobody is in the way.
- Export as USDZ and AirDrop it to Josh.

**What to scan**

- [ ] **Pub 85 room:** the whole room, for its real size, walls, doors and where the tables sit.
- [ ] **Palmer's room:** the same.
- [ ] **A whole table at each hall:** walk around it once, for the rail, apron and leg shapes.
- [ ] **A corner pocket and a side pocket:** close, slowly, the casting and rubber included.
- [ ] **A table lamp:** from below and from the side.
- [ ] **Props:** a bar stool, the chalk, a cue rack, the ball rack.

Tape measurements are still needed: LiDAR is accurate to about a centimetre, and the pockets need millimetres.
